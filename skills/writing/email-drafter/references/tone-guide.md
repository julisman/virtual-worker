# Tone ladder — registers with openers and closers (EN + ID)

Pick the lowest rung that still respects the relationship. When unsure between two
rungs, pick the more formal one for first contact and the less formal one for an
ongoing thread.

## The ladder

| Rung | Register | Use for | Signals |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Formal | Executives, officials, legal/HR matters, first contact with a senior stranger | Full titles, no contractions, ID: `Bapak/Ibu` + `Anda` |
| 2 | Professional | Clients, cross-team peers, vendors | Polite but direct, light contractions, ID: `Anda` |
| 3 | Friendly-professional | Teammates, known clients, ongoing threads | First names, contractions fine, ID: `Anda` or name |
| 4 | Casual | Close colleagues, internal quick notes | Short, conversational, ID: `kamu` |

## Openers by rung

**English**
1. "Dear Mr./Ms. [Name]," → "I am writing to…"
2. "Hello [Name]," → "I hope you're doing well. I'm reaching out about…"
3. "Hi [Name]," → "Quick note about…" / "Thanks for your message —"
4. "Hey [Name]," → "Quick one:" / "Heads up —"

**Indonesian**
1. "Yth. Bapak/Ibu [Nama]," → "Bersama email ini, kami bermaksud…"
2. "Selamat pagi/siang Bapak/Ibu [Nama]," → "Perkenalkan, saya… Saya menghubungi Anda terkait…"
3. "Halo [Nama]," → "Semoga sehat selalu. Saya ingin menindaklanjuti…"
4. "Halo [Nama]!" → "Mau tanya cepat soal…" / "Sekadar info ya —"

## Closers by rung

**English**
1. "Thank you for your time and consideration. Sincerely, [Name]"
2. "Thank you — I look forward to your reply. Best regards, [Name]"
3. "Thanks! Let me know if you have questions. Best, [Name]"
4. "Thanks! [Name]"

**Indonesian**
1. "Atas perhatian dan kerja sama Bapak/Ibu, kami ucapkan terima kasih. Hormat kami, [Nama]"
2. "Terima kasih atas perhatiannya. Saya menantikan kabar dari Anda. Salam, [Nama]"
3. "Terima kasih ya. Kabari saya kalau ada pertanyaan. Salam, [Nama]"
4. "Makasih ya! [Nama]"

## Register notes

- **Indonesian formality is binary in practice**: rungs 1–3 use `Anda` (capitalized) or
  the person's name/title; rung 4 uses `kamu`. Never mix `Anda` and `kamu` in one email.
- English rung 1 avoids contractions entirely ("do not", "we are").
- Requests soften going up the ladder: rung 1 "Would it be possible to…" / ID
  "Apakah memungkinkan untuk…" · rung 4 "Can you…" / ID "Bisa tolong…".
- Bad-news emails: go one rung more formal than the relationship suggests, state the
  news early, and avoid hedging that buries it.
